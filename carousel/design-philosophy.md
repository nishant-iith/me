# Design Philosophy — "Structured Ink"

A discipline of black on white: engineering rigor rendered as typography, structured like a
well-written commit. White space is load-bearing — it is the reviewer's reading rhythm. Type is
the only material: sometimes architectural and bold (Big Shoulders controlling a headline the way
a stack map commands a page), sometimes monospaced and clinical (JetBrains Mono — the typewriter
of the terminal), once a serif whisper (Libre Baskerville) for the human aside.

Harsh line rules — 2pt black bars — act like diffs themselves: additions, boundaries, separators.
No greys heavier than 60%. Nothing decorative. Every glyph earns its place. The craft must appear
labored over: optically aligned, mathematically spaced, the product of countless hours of patience.
Numbered steps, layer diagrams, monospace code fragments — the spare signage of systems engineering,
composed with the restraint of Swiss formalism and the confidence of someone who deletes more than
they add. Minimal, brutal, readable at a glance on a phone screen.
